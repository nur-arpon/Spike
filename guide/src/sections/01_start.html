Chapter 1

Before you start

Read this chapter once, all the way through, before you touch a soldering iron. It lists the tools, the safety rules for the battery and the iron, and how every step in this guide is laid out.

1.1What you are building

Desk Buddy is a small robot dog about 17 cm wide. Each of its four legs has a hip servo and a knee servo, and each paw has a wheel servo, so it can walk slowly or roll. A tail servo wags. Its face is a 4.3 inch touch screen, its eyes are a camera in the muzzle, and it hears you through two microphones.

You will build it in this order: print the parts, check every bought part, build the wiring harness, test everything on the desk, assemble the body, lid, head, legs, tail and ears, and finally switch it on and calibrate it.

Plan on roughly two days of printing at the maker space, an evening for the harness, an evening for the bench test and a weekend for the assembly. Nothing here is hard, but almost everything is small, so work slowly and in good light.

1.3Safety

Soldering and fumes

The iron tip is about 350 degrees C. Always put it back in its stand, never on the bench. Work by an open window or with a small fan pulling the smoke away from your face, not towards it. Wash your hands after soldering and before you eat. Wear glasses when you trim wire ends, because they fly.

18650 cells

These cells hold a lot of energy. Never let anything metal touch both ends of a cell: no loose cells in a pocket or a bag with keys or coins. Never use a cell with a torn or dented wrapper. Check the + and - marks twice before a cell goes into the holder, every single time. Never mix the Samsung cells with the spare Tinker cells, and always change both cells together.

Use the fuse

The protection board only cuts off at about 20 A, which is enough to melt thin wire inside a plastic shell. The 7.5 A fuse is what protects the robot. Never bridge it, and never replace it with a bigger one.

Power off before you touch the wiring

Switch the robot off before you plug or unplug anything. For any work inside the tub, also take the cells out. Keep your fingers away from the legs while the robot is switched on: servos are strong enough to pinch.

Heat

The printed parts are PLA, which softens at about 55 degrees C. Never leave the robot in a car or in direct sun behind a window.

Left, right, front and back

Left and right always mean the robot's own left and right, as if you were sitting on it facing forward. The front is the end with the head. So when you face the robot, its left side is on your right.

Part names

Printed parts are named by their file, for example Thigh_FrontLeft_Outer. Bought parts use the names in the Parts check chapter.

Colours

Printed parts come in three colours: {{swatch:white}}{{white}}, {{swatch:graphite}}{{graphite}} and {{swatch:black}}{{black}} (the small caps and covers). {{render_note}}

Screws into plastic

All screws are M2 self-tapping: they cut their own thread in the printed hole. Drive each screw in once before the part goes on, then take it out (this is called pre-threading). Tighten until the head touches, then a quarter turn more, and stop. Over-tightening strips the thread.
